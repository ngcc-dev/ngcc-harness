Impact: signing +1% to +2% at Phoenix-SM3-512f (estimate)
Scope: Sub-algorithm (large), specification and code

## Attacks considered

- sign-19-4: A 256-bit SM3 prehash caps message-retargeting security (Implementation, Confirmed)
- sign-19-8: SM3 fixed points extend any signed message under its own signature (Implementation, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **sign-19-4, sign-19-8.** Both findings have one root cause: `H_msg` passes the message through one plain SM3 call, and its 32-byte digest, which is also the last chaining value, is the only message-dependent input of the expansion. The costed repair computes that seed with a hash whose chaining state and output are wide enough for the level, and expands from the wider seed. This is the usual repair for a narrow hash state, and one change repairs both findings. A wider output alone, or a length prefix, would leave the 256-bit chaining state that the long-message attack of sign-19-8 targets; a cap on message length or on signatures per key would give up what the call and the specification require. Scope: Sub-algorithm, specification and code: the specification defines `H_msg` only abstractly and gives no SM3 instantiation, so it must state one, and `hash_message` changes in the sixteen SM3 trees from 192 to 512 bits. The signature encoding does not change: the seed is not transmitted, and `R` is already n bytes. Impact: no transmitted byte. Speed is counted in SM3 compression calls for the 32-byte message of the submitted benchmark. Signing evaluates `H_msg` once per forced-pruning trial, about 1 to 13,000 times, and the seed hash is 2 to 4 of the 22 to 67 compressions of a trial, at most 0.53% of signing and 0.02% of verification. Counted by calls and bytes with an ideal wide hash, as the fixing-cost rules prescribe for the contest's `pseudohash` and `pseudoXOF`, which the tree already carries (`mgf1_sm3` calls `pseudoXOF`; nothing calls `pseudohash`), only the expansion input grows: signing +0.0% to +1.0%. If the wider hash is charged two SM3 passes over the message, which is what the shipped contest `pseudohash` does at 512, 768 and 1,024 bits, signing rises by up to 1.4%; at one pass per 256 bits of state, by up to 2.1%. All three maxima are at 512f. Verification rises by at most 0.07%. These are estimates: the trial counts come from sampling. What would tip the answer is message length. Signing hashes the whole message in every trial, so on longer messages the result follows the per-byte cost of the wider hash. By calls and bytes nothing changes at any length. At two SM3 passes the added hashing reaches 10% of signing at about 4 to 32 kB in seven sets and 10% of verification at about 78 to 668 kB. The header does not include this, because the stated timings are for 32-byte messages. The report records no proposed fix. Not checked: nothing was built or run; the AVX2 trees were not counted; the counter search of GWOTS+C and `PRF_msg` are left out of the counts; no wide hash is named or evaluated here.
