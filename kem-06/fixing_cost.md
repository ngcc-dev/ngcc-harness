## Attacks considered

- kem-06-4: Ignored padding bits make ciphertexts malleable without changing the key (Implementation, Confirmed)
- kem-06-8: Transposing the ideal code puts maximal-minors key recovery below the BRA targets (Design, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **kem-06-4.** Root cause: each packed vector ends with 5 unused bits that the parser drops (`rbc_vec.c:789-809`), and decapsulation then compares and hashes re-serialized copies of the parsed `u` and `v` (`kem.c:241-252`, `kem.c:265-266`). The received bytes are never compared. The costed repair is the usual one for a non-canonical encoding, which is to reject it: `bra_decaps` compares the received bytes of `u` and `v` with the serialized re-encryption and hashes the received bytes into `K`. A ciphertext with a nonzero unused bit then takes the implicit-rejection branch. This is the comparison of Algorithm 9, line 4 (physical p. 15), applied to the byte string. The specification fixes the vector length at ⌈mn/8⌉ bytes (Table 3 note, physical p. 16) and has no rule for the unused bits, so it gains one encoding rule: the unused bits are zero and the comparison is on bytes. Scope: Function, specification and code; the code change is confined to `bra_decaps`, on the same lines in all six trees. Impact: none. Honest ciphertexts are unchanged, because the serializer already writes zero in the unused bits (`rbc_vec.c:761`). Decapsulation compares the same 2·⌈mn/8⌉ bytes and hashes an input of the same length. The code did not skip specified work at this step: it performs the Algorithm 9 comparison and both hash calls, on the wrong bytes. The report records no proposed fix. Not checked: nothing was built or run; the optimized trees were read by source only.
- **kem-06-8.** Root cause: `P(X)` is irreducible over F_2, so the key relation `s = x + h·y` is also a rank-decoding instance over F_(2^n) with two blocks of length m, and n is nearly twice m in every set. The report records no proposed fix. The costed repair is new parameters with the algorithms kept, the usual repair for an attack cost below target. A ring without the F_(2^n) structure would be a Construction change, and none is proposed. Scope: Parameter set, specification and code: Table 2, Table 3 and the security tables, and in the code `parameters.h`, a field library for each new m and a ring modulus for each new n. The estimate uses the finding's model (ordinary MM and ZD-MM at the specification's ω = 2) and searches m, n, k and the key weights w_x, w_y under the specification's own rules for the decoder, the failure rate and the plaintext space. The weight of `e` is kept at 8, its value in every submitted set. The ciphertext weights w_r1, w_r2 are costed in two ways. Low end, a floor and not a repair: w_r1, w_r2 equal to the key weights. The transposed relation `u = r1 + h·r2` must reach the target in the same model as the key relation, and this choice gives the smallest decoding weight r. The smallest sets found change each weight by at most one: public key and ciphertext +1.5% at BRA-128 and +20% at BRA-256 and BRA-512. Each of them lowers one ciphertext weight by one, which the specification's three-block ciphertext instance ("Attack-3n", Table 6) does not allow: in that instance's MM model the low-end sets give 2^207.7 at BRA-256 and 2^445.8 at BRA-512, below the targets (2^172.5 at BRA-128). The low end therefore drops out as a repair. It stays a floor for repairs that keep the algorithms and w_e: no ciphertext weights give a smaller r, and Attack-3n only adds a constraint. High end, the costed repair: the submitted w_r1, w_r2. The smallest sets found raise the key weights by one or two and pay for the larger r with larger m and n: +15% at BRA-128, +30% and +31% at BRA-256, +31% at BRA-512. With the submitted key weights as well, the search finds nothing cheaper than 28.5% more bits at BRA-128 and nothing within 75% at the other two sets. At the high-end sets Attack-3n stays at 2^193.6, 2^311.1 and 2^546.3, and the AGHT and PRR attacks of Table 7 at 2^253.4 or more on the key instance and 2^389.4 or more on the three-block instance. Impact (estimate): public key and ciphertext +15% at BRA-128 and +30% to +31% at BRA-256 and BRA-512; key generation, encapsulation and decapsulation +33%, +30%, +24% at BRA-128, +28%, +28%, +42% at BRA-256 and +60%, +58%, +84% at BRA-512. At BRA-512 most of the added work comes from the field element growing from two to three 64-bit words. The floor of the low end is +1.5% at BRA-128 and +20% at BRA-256 and BRA-512 in size, and none at BRA-128; +1%, none, +6% at BRA-256; +52%, +50%, +62% at BRA-512 in work, so the answer does not depend on which ciphertext weights a repair picks. These are zero-margin estimates for the weights searched, and the sets are costing aids, not recommended parameters. Ciphertext weights between the two ends, such as lowering only w_r1 where Attack-3n has room, were not searched, so a credible repair between the floor and the high end may exist. Two things would lower the estimate. At ω = 3, the exponent most favourable to the candidate, the low end is no growth at BRA-128 and BRA-256 and +7% in size with 30% to 36% more work at BRA-512; the high end is +15% at BRA-256 and +22% at BRA-512; the ω = 3 sets were not checked against Attack-3n or Table 7. If the weight of `e` were also cut from 8 to 1, the low end at ω = 2 would be +6% at BRA-256 and +10% at BRA-512; that is a floor and not a repair, because the three-block instance that this weight protects is not modeled. Not checked: the OJ attack of Table 7, which `fixing_cost_attack3n.py` does not model and which is not the cheapest attack at any submitted set; Attack-3n beyond the searched guesses and punctures (up to 2 punctures per block, where the BRA-128 optimum sits); ZD-MM with punctures; key weights outside the searched window; ciphertext weights other than the two choices; whether a sparse irreducible `P(X)` exists for each new n. Nothing was measured.
