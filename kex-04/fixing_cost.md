## Attacks considered

- kex-04-3: DKEX-512 authentication has only a 256-bit key-generation space (Design, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **kex-04-3.** The root cause is that DKEX-512 claims the 512-bit level and authenticates both parties with ML-DSA-87, whose key generation starts from a 32-byte seed and which the specification itself rates at about 256 classical bits. The costed repair is the one the specification names: substitute a signature that reaches the 512-bit level, with no change to the key-exchange layer (physical pp. 10, 22 and 37). The report records no other proposal. Widening only the 32-byte seed is not costed as the repair. It would remove the 2^256 seed search, but the specification states that the authentication of DKEX-512 with ML-DSA-87 is limited to about 256 bits, so the level that the call requires would still not be kept. Withdrawing DKEX-512 is not a repair, because 512 bits is a required level.

  Scope: Parameter set, specification and code. Algorithms 15 to 19 stay. The `Sig` entry of the parameter set π512 (physical p. 10) and Tables 1, 4 and 5 change, as do the vendored signature code and the lengths in `adkex_sig.h`. The lengths of `pk_A`, `pk_B`, `m2` and `m3` change. The specification says that no standardized signature reaches the level, so the parameter set has to be new; none was derived for this note.

  Impact: no cost model for a 512-bit signature is available, so the relative-deficit rule of `security/FIXING_COST_ANALYSIS.md` is used. The target is 512 bits and the reported attack costs at most 2^256, a deficit of 256 bits or 50% of the target, so the security exponent has to double. The scaling law assumed is that the exponent grows linearly in the module dimension and the sizes linearly with it. The submission's own two signature sets show the law: from ML-DSA-44 at the 128-bit level to ML-DSA-87 at the 256-bit level the public key grows by a factor 1.976 and the signature by 1.912 (Table 4, physical p. 23). Applying the same factors once more gives a public key of about 5,121 bytes and a signature of about 8,847 bytes. The long-term public key then grows by 98%, message 3 by 91%, message 2 by 55% and the total transmitted by 54% (judgment). Signing and verification work scales with the same dimensions: over the last doubling the matrix grew from 16 to 56 polynomials (a factor 3.5) and the vectors from 8 to 15 (a factor 1.875), so one more doubling is taken as +88% to +250% (judgment). Pass 2 signs once, pass 3 verifies and signs, and the responder's derivation verifies. That derivation is one verification and one KDF call over 128 bytes, so the step as a whole moves by the verification figure; the share of signature work in passes 2 and 3 of the reference build was not counted. In the Cortex-M4 stage counts of Table 8 (physical p. 25), pass 3 takes 471,785 cycles at DKEX-512 against 98,605 for pass 1, which has no signature operation, so signature work is the larger part of these steps in that build. DKEX-128 and DKEX-256 are not affected.

  The figures are a lower bound of the kind a parameter estimate gives: designers add margin, and the rejection rate, the challenge space and the modulus of a larger set were not examined. Not checked: whether a signature of another family would be smaller at this level, and the specification's remark that the signature may be replaced by one selected in the NGCC program. Nothing was measured.
