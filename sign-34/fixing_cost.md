## Attacks considered

- sign-34-5: An unsalted 256-bit prehash permits signature transfer at the higher levels (Implementation, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **sign-34-5.** Make the code compute the target as `H(salt || M || pk)`, as Algorithm 8, line 4 and Algorithm 14, line 3 specify (physical pp. 15 and 20): the salt is absorbed first, inside the salt loop, in place of the fixed value `SM3(M || h)` that is computed before the salt is drawn. Under the finding's model a collision prepared in advance then does not apply, because the signer draws the salt of λ + 80 bits afterwards; the transfer needs a second preimage of the intermediate value under that salt, about 2^256 for a 256-bit value. That is the level of YuanYang.DSA-1024. YuanYang.DSA-2048 claims 512 bits and also needs the intermediate value to be 512 bits wide, or the XOF to absorb `salt || M || pk` itself. The costed construction at 2048 is v = H512(salt || M || pk), costed by its dimensions as an iterated hash with a 512-bit chaining value that absorbs 64 bytes per compression call, as SM3 does, followed by the challenge XOF on v alone, which v already binds to the salt. At 1024 it is SM3(salt || M || pk), with the challenge XOF on salt || v as now. Scope: Function, code: the two hash helpers and their call order (`common.c:33–54,57–136`, `sign.c:596,619,628` and `vrfy.c:116,127` in the yuanyang-2048 reference tree, and the same code in the other trees). The specification already has this order and stays. The signature encoding does not change, since the salt is already sent and the stated signature sizes include it. Impact, for the 64-byte benchmark message, in compression calls: verification goes from 176 to 176 at 1024 and from 356 to 357 at 2048 (+0.3%), which is none. In signing the hash of `M || pk` moves into the salt loop and runs 1.1 times per signature instead of once: 190.2 to 193.6 calls at 1024 (+1.8%) and 385.0 to 392.7 at 2048 (+2.0%) (estimate). These are increases in the hashing part only. Lattice sampling and the sampler's random-byte generation are not counted, so the increase in the whole of signing is smaller, but it is not shown to be below 1%. Feeding salt || v to the challenge XOF at 2048 instead would cost more with the placeholder as built, which hashes its whole input for every 32 output bytes: 140 input bytes are 3 blocks instead of 2, 435 calls instead of 290, about +43% of the hashing in signing and +41% in verification. That variant is not the cheapest one. For a long message the hash of `M` dominates signing, and it then runs 1.1 times per signature instead of once, approaching +10%; the rules compare a one-block message, and verification hashes it once, as submitted. Not checked: the optimized trees beyond the call order.
