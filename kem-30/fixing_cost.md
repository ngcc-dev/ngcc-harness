## Attacks considered

- kem-30-1: Re-encryption rejection sampling leaks the decrypted-message class (Side-channel, Probable)

## Remediation

- **kem-30-1.** `sample_screened_poly` repeats until the spectral bound holds (`pke.c:30–42`), and Algorithm 7 (physical p. 13) specifies the same unbounded loop. Re-encryption in decapsulation calls it for the 2k = 4 polynomials of `r` and `e1` (`pke.c:166–167`), so its work depends on the decrypted message. The costed repair is the technique that the finding names, fixed-work sampling: always generate and check 2k + s candidates, and select the first 2k accepted ones without branching. The result equals that of the submitted loop unless more than s candidates are rejected. To keep correctness, s is the smallest value for which this overflow is no more likely than the failure rate that the specification claims for the set. Scope: Sub-algorithm, specification and code. The sampler is rewritten, and Algorithm 7 gains a candidate bound and an overflow rule. No transmitted object changes. Impact: each spare candidate is one XOF call and one FFT bound check, +3.5% to +7% of decapsulation. The count gives s = 29, 24, 42, 55 and 88, and decapsulation +120% to +150% at PolarLAC-Light, +100% to +140% at -128, +170% to +180% at -256, +190% to +370% at -512 and +350% to +370% at -512* (estimate). Each range runs between the two classes of work counted, integer multiplications and hashed bytes. Encapsulation is unchanged if it keeps its early exit, which exposes only the rejection count of its own message. Key generation is unchanged. Proposed fix: the report records that the team proposes removing data-dependent rejection sampling. Its 2026-10-04 post (the confirmation linked from kem-30-1, read on 2026-11-24) says only that it will revise the encryption implementation to run in constant time by removing the data-dependent rejection sampling procedure; it names no sampler, bound or parameter and links no code. The other team posts in the thread up to 2026-11-22 add nothing on this point. If the proposal is a fixed-work sampler, it is the repair costed here. If it drops the screening of `r` and `e1`, the failure analysis relies on that screening (physical pp. 13 and 26) and would have to be redone; that was not costed. Sensitivity: three spare candidates already exceed 10% in every set. The specification advises against using the looser threshold T of Table 2 as the real threshold (physical p. 26); even with T, the count gives at most 7 to 20 spares, +29% to +83%. The answer would change only if the overflow did not have to be negligible (s of 2 or less). That needs a change to the transform, such as an encapsulation that redraws its message when a candidate is rejected. The report records no such proposal, so it is not a basis for the answer.
