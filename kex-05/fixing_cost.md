No: correcting the transition sign of the embedded Shuttle signer and deriving the wrapper identities from the verification keys leave every message and operation of LoomKEX-128, -256 and -512 less than 10% above the submission.

Impact: Confirm +4% to +8% at LoomKEX-128, -256 and -512 (judgment); key generation and AuthResp +1% to +2% at LoomKEX-128, -256 and -512 (judgment); messages 3 and 4 +0.6% at LoomKEX-512 (count)
Scope: Function (small), specification and code
Assessed: 2026-11-23

## Attacks considered

- kex-05-3: Shuttle signing-key leakage enables permanent responder impersonation (Design, Confirmed)
- kex-05-7: Hard-wired wrapper identities let a registered party misbind honest sessions (Implementation, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **kex-05-3.** The root cause is the reversed sign of the rejection-free transition in the embedded signer. Loom §2.2.8 (physical p. 12) makes the Shuttle specification [16] normative for SIG. Its Algorithm 22 and `sig/irs.c:382–399` return `y+v` on the selected interval, where its Figure 1 and Eq. (8) define `y−v`. The costed repair is the recorded proposal, to correct the sign: one statement of Algorithm 22 and one of `R_transition` (`irs.c:399`, or `:382` with `:396`; `:576` in the optimized trees). Scope: Single line, specification and code. The specification text that changes is in [16], not in the Loom document. Impact: none. The signature stays a fixed-length string of 1183/2417/5001 bytes, and keys and messages are unchanged. Each signing attempt runs exactly τ transitions of the same work for either sign (`irs.c:443–451`). Signing restarts on the norm gate and on a failed sigEncode (`sign.c:578–581, 602–610`), so the sign can change the expected number of attempts. The one-step calculation under Evidence gives the submitted sign the wider response, so the corrected signer is not expected to restart more often than the submitted code (judgment). The proposal is the Shuttle team's view as relayed by the Loom team. It is quoted as reported and was not evaluated. Restart rates were not measured or computed for either sign, and no revised specification or code was seen.
- **kex-05-7.** The root cause is in the six ICCS wrappers. They pass the constants `"initiator"` and `"responder"` as each party's own identity and as the expected peer identity (`KEX_LoomKEX-*.c:19–20, 144, 177, 347, 398`), so the tag binds no party. Algorithms 4 and 5 (physical p. 8) take each party's identity as an input, Algorithm 6 (physical p. 9) checks the tag over the identity it receives, and `loom.c` accepts per-party identities, so the protocol itself stays. The costed repair is the one the report names: derive each identity in the wrapper from the verification key that the interface passes, for example as a hash of `pka` or `pkb`. Scope: Function, specification and code: the four wrapper call sites, and at LoomKEX-512 the identity length (`loom/params.h:47`) with Tables 15 and 16. Impact on sizes: a 32-byte digest fits the existing identity field (2 + 32 bytes, Table 16), so no message changes at LoomKEX-128 and -256. At LoomKEX-512 a 32-byte identity is shorter than the level, because a second key with the same digest costs about 2^256 key generations; a 64-byte digest widens the field by 32 bytes, and messages 3 and 4 grow from 5101 to 5133 bytes, +0.6% (count). With digests of the specification's collision-resistance length b_cr (Table 3: 64 and 128 bytes) the growth is +1.3% at LoomKEX-256 and +1.9% at LoomKEX-512 (count). Impact on speed: each party hashes its own key once, at key generation, and the peer's key once per session. Confirm (`kex_derive_ss_a`) is the smallest operation that needs the peer identity: one signature verification and one MAC check. Verification already hashes the whole verification key once (`sign.c:700–702`) and hashes or squeezes at least 9548/13964/27916 bytes in all. One more pass over the 1264/1952/3648-byte key adds 13% to 14% to that byte count. The NTT, decoding and hint work of verification does not change, so Confirm grows by less: +4% to +8% (judgment). The stated timings of the SHAKE build put the added pass at no more than 7.6%/7.4%/6.8% of Confirm (estimate, see Evidence). Key generation and AuthResp (`kex_generate_pass4_msg_b`) each hash one key more and grow by +1% to +2% (judgment; at most 1.8% and 1.4% by the same bound). Not checked: the specification gives no breakdown of verification time, so the lower end of the Confirm range is not derived. Which hash the wrapper would use was not fixed; the count assumes one pass at the cost per byte of the existing key hash. The submitted ICCS build replaces SHAKE with the contest's SM3-based functions (`loom/symmetric-iccs.c:50–103`) and is not the build that the specification timed.

- Control: Table 16 (physical p. 53) builds message 3 as (2 + 32) + (2 + |σ|) + |τ| = 34 + 1185 + 16 = 1235, 34 + 2419 + 32 = 2485 and 34 + 5003 + 64 = 5101. With a 64-byte identity at LoomKEX-512: 66 + 5003 + 64 = 5133, +32 bytes, +0.63%; the four messages go from 16170 to 16234 bytes, +0.40%. With b_cr-length identities: 2485 + 32 = 2517 (+1.29%) and 5101 + 96 = 5197 (+1.88%).
- Identity field: `LOOM_MAX_IDBYTES 32` (`loom/params.h:47`), enforced in `loom/loom.c:107–111, 124`. The wrapper lines are the same in all six trees.
- Bytes hashed or squeezed by one verification (`sig/sign.c:640–785`; constants in `loom/sigparams.h:58–105, 157–192`), for LoomKEX-128/-256/-512. HashPK, 1 + |pk|: 1265 / 1953 / 3649. HashMsg, 1 + (32, 64, 128) + |DTBS|, where |DTBS| = 9 + |ek| + |ct| = 1497 / 2665 / 5833 (`loom/loom.c:33–64`): 1530 / 2730 / 5962. ExpandA, at least 2 bytes for each of (m + m·ℓ)·n = 3072 / 4096 / 8192 coefficients (`sig/polyvec.c:253–297`): 6144 / 8192 / 16384. HashCh, 1 + m·(n·d_h/8 + n/8) + (32, 64, 128): 609 / 1089 / 1921. Sum: 9548 / 13964 / 27916.
- One more pass over |pk|: 1264/9548 = 13.2%, 1952/13964 = 14.0%, 3648/27916 = 13.1%. SampleC, rejected ExpandA candidates and the MAC are left out of the sum, so these ratios are upper bounds.
- Stated timings: Table 13 (physical p. 51) lists KeyGen at 626,614/929,680/1,817,397 cycles, AuthResp at 781,698/1,342,873/2,692,192 cycles and Confirm at 149,420/229,245/447,903 cycles for the reference C code. §5.1 (physical p. 49) says that they were taken with the SHAKE-256 symmetric branch. Nothing was measured here.
- Bound from the stated timings: Table 11 (physical p. 49) gives 63,262 cycles for the reference Loom-KEM-128 key generation. In the SHAKE build that operation runs at least 56 Keccak permutations: 1 to expand the seed, 2 for each of the 25 matrix polynomials and 1 for each of the 5 noise polynomials (`kem/indcpa.c:153, 232–257, 281–286`, `kem/poly.c:116–121`, `loom/fips202.c:500–514`). One permutation therefore costs at most 63,262/56 = 1,130 cycles. A SHAKE256 pass over 1 + |pk| bytes is 10/15/27 permutations, at most 11,300/16,950/30,510 cycles. Against Table 13 that is 7.6%/7.4%/6.8% of Confirm, 1.4%/1.3%/1.1% of AuthResp and 1.8%/1.8%/1.7% of KeyGen.
- Transition sign: let ρ_k be the Gaussian weight of the point y + k·v on the line through y, with v oriented so that ⟨y,v⟩ > 0, and let A_k = ρ_k − ρ_(k+1) + ρ_(k+2) − …. Algorithm 22 selects its interval with probability A_k/ρ_k. Stepping back on that event (`y−v`, as Figure 1 and Eq. (8) define) sends mass (ρ_(k−1) − A_(k−1)) + A_(k+1) = ρ_k to point k, which leaves the weights as they were. Stepping forward on it (`y+v`, as submitted) sends ρ_(k−1) − ρ_k + ρ_(k+1), whose second moment along v is larger by 2‖v‖². The submitted code therefore adds about 2‖v‖² to E‖z‖² at each of the τ = 42/58/115 transitions, and the corrected code adds none. Points next to the origin, where the orientation of v changes, are ignored.
