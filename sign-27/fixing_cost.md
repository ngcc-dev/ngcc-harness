## Attacks considered

## Remediation

- **sign-27-2.** Lead; it does not decide the answer. Root cause: the primes are sized by p^(1/2) > 2^λ. The repair is a larger prime. Scope: Parameter set, specification and code. The report gives a candidate-specific cost only at λ = 128, 2^[138.78,143.51] gates against a 2^143 target. The issue's cost model that the report names (`w26_estimate.py`) is not in the repository and was not run, so the relative-deficit rule applies. The deficit is at most 4.22 bits, or 3.0% of the target. Assuming that the attack exponent and the sizes both grow linearly in log2 p, the prime grows from 250.3 to about 258 bits, the public key from 65 to 67 bytes (+3.1%) and the signature from 204 to 211 (+3.4%) (judgment). The report also cites the roughly 326-bit prime of [SQIsign v3](https://sqisign.org/spec/sqisign-20260901.pdf) at this level as parameter-selection evidence, not as an attack threshold; at 326 bits the size formulas give 83 (+28%) and 247 (+21%). Taken with sign-27-3, whose costed prime has 3λ bits (384 at λ = 128), no further change is costed, so the With leads line repeats the main one.
- **sign-27-3.** Root cause: one congruence q ≡ c2 (mod c1) with a λ/2-bit prime c1 binds the message, so a fixed response satisfies a random challenge with probability about 2^(-λ/2). The costed repair is the usual one for a challenge space below the level, a wider challenge: a λ-bit prime c1 and a λ-bit c2. The response degree depends on c1. EquivalentSpecialIdeal searches a coset of a sublattice of index c1^3 in a rank-4 lattice (Algorithm 3.13 line 14, physical p. 34; `quaternion/ref/generic/ideal.c:903–911`), so log2 q ≈ (1/2) log2 p + (3/2) log2 c1. This matches the submitted q field of 1.75λ bits (Table 9) and the exponents that the report observed, for example 211 to 219 against a predicted 221 at λ = 128. The specification's own failure analysis states q ≤ O(p^(1/2)) (physical pp. 46–47), which matches neither. With a λ-bit c1 the law gives log2 q ≈ 317 at λ = 128, above f = 248, and both signer and verifier need e_rsp ≤ f (`signature/ref/lvlx/sign.c:89`, `verification/ref/lvlx/verify.c:23`). The smallest prime that keeps e_rsp ≤ f has about 3λ bits. Scope: Parameter set, specification and code: new primes and precomputed constants, the challenge width in the hash-to-challenge routine, and every encoded width; the challenge space of the security proof changes as well. Impact at zero margin: public key +49% to +50% and signature +65% at all four sets; verification x3.6 to x4.1 (estimate); signing x2.8 to x4.8 and key generation x2.4 to x4.2, scaled from Table 10 (judgment). This is a zero-margin estimate that keeps the submitted response algorithm. The report records no proposed fix for this finding.
- **sign-27-5.** Root cause: the verifier never checks that the response isogeny has the declared degree q, so q and the auxiliary basis can be rescaled together without changing the kernel. The costed repair is the submitters' proposal of 2026-09-29, quoted as proposed and not evaluated: check the actual response degree on the challenged codomain factor with Weil pairings of its torsion-point images and the degree bound, and hash a canonical encoding of the commitment. Scope: Function, specification and code (one step in Algorithm 4.3 and in `verify.c`; the source already has `weil`, `ec/ref/lvlx/biextension.c:221`, and point evaluation in the (2,2)-chain, `hd/ref/lvlx/theta_isogenies.c:1058`). Impact: two Weil pairings of order 2^e_rsp, three more points pushed through the chain and one exponentiation add 16% to 18% to verification (estimate). No size changes, and signing does not change. The submitted code already hashes j-invariants (`verify.c:64`), so the encoding clarification costs nothing. Combined with sign-27-3, verification is x4.3 to x4.8.
