## Remediation

- **sign-21-1, sign-21-2.** Both findings have one root cause. Within one signature every `TCCR(r, s, iv)` call of the pcGGM tree uses the same `(s, iv)`, and every `LeafHash` call is `PRG(x, iv, 0^8; 2λ)`, so one guess is tested against all revealed nodes or all hidden-leaf commitments. The costed repair gives each call its own tweak: the node index `id` for TCCR in BAVC.Commit, BAVC.Open and BAVC.Verify, and the leaf index for the PRG call in LeafHash. It is the first fix in the original post, which names both TCCR and the leaf PRG, and the team accepts it for TCCR ("using node indices for the TCCR tweaks (same as FAEST v2)"); the report notes that a change to the tree expansion alone does not separate `LeafHash`. The multi-target gain comes from shared hash inputs and not from the regular-syndrome-decoding problem, so no parameter changes. Scope: Function, specification and code: the tweak that BAVC.Commit, BAVC.Open and BAVC.Verify pass to TCCR and that LeafHash (§4.4.7) passes to PRG, with the index passed at their call sites; TCCR and PRG themselves (§4.9.8, §4.9.9) are unchanged. The reference tree expander already holds `id`, and its PRG has a tweak argument that LeafHash sets to 0; the optimized code computes the node index and receives leaf tweaks, and discards them. The proposals do not fix where the index enters the cipher input, so the costed encoding is this: every call passes the tweak iv ⊕ IntToBits(id; 120) where it now passes `iv`, with id ∈ [1, 2L) the position of the node or leaf in the large tree (leaves at [L, 2L)). TCCR keeps its key x_R ‖ tweak ‖ 0^(n_k + n_b − 128 − λ) and its plaintext i ‖ x_L, and the counter-mode PRG keeps its block tweak ‖ (t + i) ‖ 0^(n_b − 128). For one signature the map id ↦ iv ⊕ id is one-to-one, so no two calls at different positions share a tweak, and each tweak stays uniform over 2^120 values. XORing a counter into a fixed random IV is the usual per-record nonce construction (RFC 8446, §5.3); how FAEST v2, which the team cites, encodes its tweak was not checked. Impact: none (count). The verifier already computes every position, so nothing is added to the signature, and no cipher call or block is added: TCCR still makes ⌈λ/n_b⌉ = 2 calls under one key, and LeafHash still makes ⌈2λ/n_b⌉ = 3, 4 and 4 counter-mode blocks at 160, 256 and 512 and two TCCR evaluations at 384. The index needs 13 to 18 bits. At 160 and 512 it would also fit, without XOR, in the zero padding of the TCCR key (32 and 128 bits) and of the PRG key (32 bits at 160) or block (128 bits at 512), at the same call count. At 256 and 384 the key x_R ‖ iv and the plaintext i ‖ x_L are full (n_k + n_b = λ + 128), and the byte i leaves 7 spare bits, so a concatenated (iv, id) would need a further cipher call or a wider cipher; that variant is not the cheapest and is not costed. Theorem 8.12 (physical p. 110) bounds the TCCR advantage by 4 q_E µ/2^ρ + q_C(µ − 1)/2^n, with µ the largest number of construction queries on one tweak. The submission has µ = 3L/2 − 1 in every signature. With the XOR encoding a tweak value can repeat only between signatures: at 2^64 signatures, three positions share one with expected count at most 2^2.0 and four with at most 2^−38.5 (512s), so µ is at most 3 positions, or 6 queries since a bottom node makes two. A concatenated encoding would give 2 positions (4 queries), as the iv itself repeats about 2^7 times among 2^64 signatures. The term therefore changes by a small constant, not by the factor of up to 3L/2 that the finding exploits; a full proof under the encoding was not checked. The post's alternative, raising λ by ceil(log2(T_open)) + 1 bits, is not costed. It is a Parameter set change, and the specified ciphers leave no room for it: PRG needs n_k ≥ λ, which AES-256 and SHACAL-2 meet with equality at 256 and 512, and TCCR needs n_k + n_b ≥ λ + 128, which Rijndael-256 meets with equality at 384.
