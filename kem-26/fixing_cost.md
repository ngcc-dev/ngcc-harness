## Remediation

- **kem-26-2, kem-26-3, kem-26-7.** One new parameter table for NSS-HQC-256, -384 and -512 is the repair costed for all three; the algorithms, `Q = 4` and `mult = 5` stay, and NSS-HQC-128 is not changed. The report records no proposed fix. Weights (kem-26-3, kem-26-7): `w_r1 = w_r2` rises from 106, 159, 213 to 122, 185, 249 (+15.1%, +16.4%, +16.9%), the smallest values at which the report's Stern cost reaches the target after the `sqrt(n)` discount. `w_sk` rises from 175, 233 to 181, 245 at the 384- and 512-bit sets, without the discount as in kem-26-7 (185, 249 with it). A longer ring adds little, since the report's costs exceed the weight by 22 to 24 bits at all four ring lengths. A sparse product costs `w·n` inner-loop steps in the submitted code, so the weights alone make encapsulation +15.1% to +16.9% slower (estimate). They also raise the bit error rate, so `n` and `n1` cannot stay. Failure bound (kem-26-2): the specification selects every set under a failure rate below 2^-128 (§3.6). The repaired sets are held to that bound under a model that counts the compressor's errors: decryption uses only the majority bit of each five-bit group, which leaves 0, 1 or 2 wrong bits per group before the structural errors. The model gives 2^-10.8 to 2^-14.0 for the submitted NSS-HQC-256, where the report records 1.4·10^-4 observed, or 2^-12.8. At the new weights no outer length that fits the submitted ring reaches the bound, so `n` grows too. The smallest ciphertext found has `n` 54,493 → 75,533 to 80,021 and `n1` 70 → 110 to 114 at NSS-HQC-256, `n` +27% to +37% and `n1` 100 → 180 to 195 at NSS-HQC-384, and `n` +25% to +32% and `n1` 130 → 254 at NSS-HQC-512. The two ends of each range are outer decoding with threshold erasures and with errors only. Scope: Parameter set, specification and code (Tables 1–4 and 15, the failure analysis of §3, `params.h`, and the 130-symbol limit `RS_MAX_N` at `code_layer.c:7–8`). Lengths change; no encoding changes. Impact (estimate) at NSS-HQC-256, -384, -512: public key +38% to +47%, +27% to +37%, +25% to +32%; ciphertext +44% to +49%, +39% to +44%, +35% to +40%; key generation +39% to +47%, +32% to +41%, +32% to +39%; encapsulation +60% to +69%, +48% to +59%, +47% to +54%; decapsulation +56% to +60%, +54% to +59%, +48% to +55%. The figures have no margin and are a lower bound for this repair. kem-26-2 is Probable, and the answer does not rest on it: with `w_r1 = w_r2`, as in every submitted set, the weight increase comes from Confirmed findings and alone exceeds 10% in encapsulation. The report measured failures only at NSS-HQC-256; the bound is applied to the 384- and 512-bit sets because sets with heavier weights must still meet the specification's own failure bound. Alternative: send the second component uncompressed, the "baseline, no quant." column of Table 2 (physical p. 26). `n` and the public key then stay, and the model gives ciphertext +12.3%, +8.6%, +6.1% with `n1` reduced to 42, 60, 76, or +37.0%, +25.8%, +19.8% with `n1` kept. That removes modification M2 and changes the ciphertext encoding, which is further from the submitted design than the root cause requires, so it is not the costed repair; the answer is the same, because the weights still apply. Not checked: `cryptographic-estimators` 2.1.1 is not installed, so no Stern cost was recomputed; the weights come from a straight line through the two costs that the report gives at each `n`, and the relative deficits of kem-26-3 are 11.5%, 13.3% and 13.8%. The same-key model of kem-26-6 was not re-run at the new weights, which leave under 2 bits above each target for one ciphertext, so that model may ask for more weight. The model takes bit errors and blocks as independent, as §3.2 does. Other values of `mult`, which Eq. (73) fixes at 5, were not searched. Unequal `w_r1` and `w_r2`, which Table 1 lists separately, were not searched either: both products of encryption use `r2`, so raising `w_r1` alone would add no counted steps, but Eq. (63) depends on both weights, so the failure bound would still ask for the longer ring. A decoder that uses the 2-bit symbol as soft information, which the specification mentions as an option (physical p. 15), has no proposal and was not costed.
- **kem-26-4.** Derive two sub-seeds of `λ_seed` bits each. SHA3-512 gives only 512 bits, so step 5 of Algorithm 1 (physical p. 17) and step 2 of Algorithm 3 (physical p. 20) need an output of 768 or 1,024 bits, for example from the scheme's own `Expand`. Scope: Function, specification and code (`derive_xy_seeds` and `NSS_HQC_I_SEED_BYTES`, `nss_hqc_core.c:25,432–439`). Impact: none. The sub-seeds are secret and are not stored, and `seed_sk` already has `λ_seed/8` bytes in the secret key. One hash call becomes one XOF call. Not checked: whether SHAKE256's 512-bit capacity limits the 384- and 512-bit sets; the report records no finding on it.
- **kem-26-5.** Replace the trial loop by a decoder with fixed work: a syndrome decoder with fixed iteration counts, the kind that §2.6.3 names, with field arithmetic free of tables and zero tests (`gf_mul`, `code_layer.c:66–72`). Scope: Sub-algorithm, specification and code. §2.6.3 describes a decoder that the code does not use, and kem-26-9 shows that its syndromes are wrong for the shortened code. Impact: none (judgment). With no erasures, the largest case, one submitted trial is 0.4% to 0.7% of the counted steps of decapsulation. Table 9 (physical p. 42) puts the named decoder at `O(n1^2)` field operations, where one submitted trial needs about `n1^3/2`. Keeping the submitted solver and running every trial with a masked result would add up to about 3%, 6%, 10% and 13% to decapsulation at the four sets (estimate, one field multiplication taken as one step). The finding is Probable. Not checked: the cost of constant-time field arithmetic, and whether the inner decoder and the sampler leak.
- **kem-26-6.** State in the specification that one key serves at most 2^64 encapsulations. The report gives at least 2^128 bit operations at 2^76 ciphertexts under its model, so the attack stays at the target within the limit. The cost at exactly 2^64 was not computed: the witness needs scipy and downloads its estimator. Scope: Single line, specification. Impact: none. Alternative for 2^80 ciphertexts: by the same straight line `w_r1 = w_r2 = 67` adds 1.9 bits and 68 adds 3.8, against a shortfall of 0.4 bits at 2^77 that grows with the number of ciphertexts; encapsulation +1.5% to +3.0% (judgment). The cost at 2^80 and the failure rate at those weights were not computed.
