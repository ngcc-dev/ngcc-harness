## Attacks considered

- sign-25-1: Verifier verdict is decided by stale stack contents (Implementation, Confirmed)
- sign-25-3: Detached compact challenge enables one-query message-retargeting forgeries (Design, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **sign-25-1.** Root cause: the verification path does not enforce its point-order, isotropy and codomain conditions, so a malformed signature reaches the final comparison with unwritten values. The repair makes the code follow the specification. It initialises the values the verdict reads, rejects malformed decoded fields, and returns failure where the code now only asserts (the split result, `theta_isogenies.c:1779–1780`). It also runs the checks that the specification places in verification and that the source omits or keeps under `_DEBUG`: the order test at every step of ThreeIsogenyChain (Algorithm 7.32 line 15; `isog_chains.c:440–447`), and the splitting and isotropy tests of each (2,2)-step (Algorithm 7.36 lines 5 and 22, which "must be performed during verification", physical p. 90; absent from `theta_isogeny_comput`, `theta_isogenies.c:719–850`). This is the cheapest credible repair because the specified checks sit inside the chains: one tripling per 3-isogeny step and at most 8 multiplications per (2,2)-step. Scope: Function, code. The changes are local but fall in several routines of the verification path (`sign.c`, `basis.c`, `isog_chains.c`, `theta_isogenies.c`, `pack.c`). Impact: the code skipped specified work, so verification grows by 3.0% to 3.2% uncompressed and 2.6% to 2.8% compact (estimate); no size changes. Running in addition the order tests that the source keeps under `NDEBUG` and `assert`, as written (`basis.c:661–668,684–690`, `theta_isogenies.c:1637,1646`), would bring the total to 14% to 15% of uncompressed verification; that is not the costed repair, because the specification places its checks inside the chains (section 3.4.4, physical p. 52). Algorithm 3.3 as printed has an empty branch at line 7 (physical p. 53); correcting it is editorial and adds no work.
- **sign-25-3.** Root cause: compact verification (Algorithm 3.6, physical p. 56) derives E_com from the response and the dual path (t, g), and compares the transmitted chl only with a hash, so nothing ties chl to the challenge isogeny. The costed repair is the one the report states: use the hash-derived challenge to build the forward challenge kernel on E_com, and check its codomain and dual-kernel relation against the reconstructed path. This adds to compact verification what the uncompressed verifier already does on E_com (`sign.c:1041–1052`): a deterministic basis of E_com[3^b], the point R_com + [chl]S_com, and a 3^b-isogeny chain with its order checks, followed by comparisons. E_com is not transmitted in compact mode, so signer and verifier must first bring it to one model for the deterministic basis to agree; the code does this for E_chl with `ec_curve_standardized` (`sign.c:864,1189`), and one such call is counted. The report records no proposed fix. Scope: Sub-algorithm, specification and code. The repair replaces how the compact mode ties the challenge to the commitment, on both sides: a step is added to compact verification (Algorithm 3.6, `sign.c:1126–1265`), compact signing changes its model of E_com (Algorithm 3.5) and the derivation of the challenge kernel (the signer path below), and the compact verifier gains a second chain. That is more than one routine or one step of one algorithm, so Function understated it. Impact on verification, with sign-25-1 included, in two alternatives that are not combined; the Impact line gives the alternative with the hint, which has the smaller verification cost. With a two-byte basis hint for E_com in the signature, as the uncompressed format has, compact verification grows by 21% to 22% (estimate); that changes the signature encoding and adds 0.3% to 1.2% to its size (count). Without the hint the encoding stays, the verifier searches the basis as in Algorithm 2.3, and compact verification grows by 26% to 28% (estimate). Both exceed 10%, so no consistent choice keeps every metric under 10%. The model, the basis and the kernel point alone are 10% to 12% of compact verification, so the total stays above 10% even if the second chain could be avoided. Impact on signing: the specification's signer already takes the kernel from the deterministic basis (Algorithm 3.5 lines 13, 15, 23 and 24, physical p. 55), but the submitted compact signer does not. It uses the image of the fixed basis under the commitment isogeny (`sign.c:785–792`), which the verifier cannot rebuild. The corrected compact signer adds the steps the uncompressed signer has at `sign.c:767–784`, the basis search and the 3^b change of basis, and the model of E_com. By the same count that is 80,151 to 356,130 F_p multiplications, 39% to 40% of compact verification work (count). Signing as a whole is not counted, so no signing percentage is given; Table 6.2 shows that compact signing takes several times as long as compact verification, so the addition is a smaller share of signing, but that timing is not turned into a percentage. Key generation and the public key do not change. Not checked: the binding equality of the 2025 CompactSQIsign2D2 construction that the report mentions, which may cost differently; the comparisons that close the check, which are not counted; and whether a single-point discrete logarithm (`ec_dlog_3_tate`) could replace the change of basis in the signer.
