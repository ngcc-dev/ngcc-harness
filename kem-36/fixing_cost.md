## Attacks considered

- kem-36-9: Same-key multi-instance decoding puts three TRIKE sets below target (Design, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **kem-36-9.** Root cause: every ciphertext under one key gives r rotated targets (u, v) for one [3r, r] decoding search of weight t, and the estimate falls below target after 2^47 (TRIKE-5) and 2^63 (TRIKE-7, -9) honest ciphertexts. A stated limit alone is not a credible repair, because these counts are under 2^64: at 2^64 ciphertexts the finding's estimator gives 2^249.336, 2^383.419 and 2^511.378. The costed repair is a stated limit of 2^64 encapsulations per public key together with new TRIKE-5, -7 and -9 parameters at which the finding's estimator reaches the target at the limit. Static keys and the security notion are kept. A heavier error raises the decoding failure rate, and TRIKE-5 has no slack (2^-256.44 in Table 8). In the failure model of §3.1 the rate is Q(√n·(C(σ) − C(σ*))/α) with C(σ) = 1 − t/n, so a longer block with t/r no larger than submitted does not raise the modelled rate if the fitted α and σ* stay. Those constants are not published, so this scaling is an assumption and the failure rate was not recomputed. Under it the smallest admissible steps are (r, t) = (36373, 441), (69827, 660) and (114419, 879), from (35363, 429), (69691, 659) and (114043, 877), with estimates 2^256.418, 2^384.005 and 2^512.561. They add 2.8% to the TRIKE-5 public key and ciphertext, 0.2% at TRIKE-7 and 0.3% at TRIKE-9. The block length also fixes the work of key generation: the inversion in `gf2x.c` is a chain for the exponent r − 2, and its number of full-length multiplications depends on the bits of r − 2. It is 20 at r = 35363 and 22 at r = 36373, so key generation would run 48 multiplications against 44, +9.1% (count). The costed TRIKE-5 set is therefore the next admissible prime whose chain is no longer than the submitted one, r = 36877 with t = 442 (2^256.294). Scope: Parameter set, specification and code: one statement of the limit; the TRIKE-5, -7 and -9 rows of Tables 1 to 3 and the failure and attack tables; r, t and the decoder coefficients in `trike_params.h`, and the inversion constants in `gf2x.c`. The public key and ciphertext change length; no field is added. Impact at TRIKE-5: public key 4,453 to 4,642 bytes (+4.2%) and ciphertext 8,874 to 9,252 bytes (+4.3%) (judgment). Work: the multiplications run at a padded length of 65,536 or 131,072 bits, which the new r does not exceed, and their number does not grow: 44, 46 (from 48) and 60 in key generation, 4 in encapsulation and 2 in decapsulation. Key generation and encapsulation are almost all multiplications (Table 5 gives 10.6 times the cycles for 11 times the multiplications) and change by under 1%. The decoder passes grow with r, by 4.3%, and are most of decapsulation, which takes three times the cycles of encapsulation with half its multiplications: decapsulation +4% (judgment). The duplicate scan of the error sampler grows by 6.2%, to 97,461 comparisons, and is small. Nothing was measured. The estimate has no margin and is a lower bound; it is under 5%. The answer would change if the failure rate forced r to grow by 10%, more than three times the growth of t. The report records no proposed fix for this finding. Alternative without a limit: at 2^80 ciphertexts the estimates are 2^242.947, 2^377.059 and 2^504.721, the smallest admissible steps are (37379, 453), (70979, 671) and (115757, 890), and the public key and ciphertext grow by 5.7%, 1.8% and 1.5% (judgment). The inversion chains at these three primes are no longer than the submitted ones. TRIKE-2 stays above target (2^146.699 at 2^80). Not checked: the failure rate at the new values, whether the slack of TRIKE-7 and TRIKE-9 in Table 8 absorbs their heavier errors at unchanged r, the weak-key thresholds, the key-recovery estimates of Table 9, and the vector code of the optimized decoder. All values are costing aids, not proposed parameters.
