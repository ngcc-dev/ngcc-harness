## Attacks considered

- kem-19-2: Lore-384 and -512 emit 256-bit keys from 256-bit keygen seeds (Design, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **kem-19-2.** Root cause: `LORE_SYMBYTES` is 32 at every level (`params.h:18`), so the key-generation seed, H(m), K̄, the coins r, z and K are all 256 bits, and §3.5 (physical p. 22) says that the 256-bit SHAKE and SM3 functions are temporary references at 384 and 512 bits, to be replaced. The plaintext is not the limit. m has κ = λ = 384 and 512 bits (Table 2, physical p. 29; `kem.c:163–164`) and the submitted BCH codes carry it, so no parameter, ciphertext field or failure probability changes. The costed repair gives the seed, seedsk, H(m), K̄, r, z and K λ bits, and moves the calls that take or produce them (Hgen, the secret sampler's XOF, H(m), G and KDF) to a hash of λ-bit strength. The specification names none, so the hash is costed by its dimensions as a Keccak sponge of capacity 2λ: rate 104 bytes at Lore-384 and 72 bytes at Lore-512, against 136 for the submitted SHAKE256 calls. expandA, H(pk) and H(c) take public inputs and stay as submitted, which is the cheapest form of the repair. H(pk) and H(c) are hashes of values that both parties hold; the λ bits of K come from m through H(m), G and K̄, and H(c) enters the KDF beside K̄ or z (Algorithms 17 and 18, physical pp. 21–22). A second ciphertext with the same H(c) still has to pass the re-encryption check, which compares the whole ciphertext (`kem.c:207` in the SM3 tree). The report records no attack that uses the 256-bit width of H(pk) or H(c), and the finding's root cause does not involve them. §3.5 says that all of H, G and KDF will be replaced once a hash of the higher level is available; that is a general plan, not a proposed fix for this finding, and it is costed below as the alternative that would tip the answer. Scope: Sub-algorithm, specification and code: the hash and XOF for secret values are replaced. Impact on sizes: none. The public key holds only the public 32-byte seedA and the ciphertext holds no hash value. The built KEM secret key grows by z, 16 and 32 bytes (under 1%). Impact on speed, SHAKE backend: Keccak permutations per key generation, encapsulation and decapsulation go from 129, 167 and 170 to 143, 193 and 197 at Lore-512, and from 90, 116 and 118 to 93, 121 and 123 at Lore-384. In the model below hashing is the smaller part of each operation, so the estimate is +2% to +5%, +2% to +7% and +2% to +6% at Lore-512, and under 3% at Lore-384. The lower end weighs a Keccak word operation like one 16-bit multiply or add of the mod-t product; the upper end weighs it four times as much, a judgment that allows for a compiler vectorising the multiplication loops. Encapsulation at Lore-512 reaches 10% at a weight of 10. Alternatives: if H(pk) and H(c) also move to the wider hash, the same model gives +4% to +11%, +6% to +17% and +5% to +15% at Lore-512, which straddles 10%. If the public matrix expansion moves as well, it gives +19% to +52%, +17% to +48% and +15% to +42%. How many of the public-input calls a revised design moves is what would tip the answer. SM3 backend: H is plain SM3-256 (`kem.c:31–33`, `sm3.c:4–7`), and G, the KDF, Hgen, the samplers and the matrix expansion are the contest's `pseudoXOF` (`sm3.c:9–15`, `sm3_xof.c`, `symmetric-sm3.c`). The repair moves H(m) to a λ-bit hash, costed like the SHAKE case as a Keccak sponge of capacity 2λ at 1.8 to 3.6 SM3 compressions per permutation (word-operation count), and widens the seeds, z, K̄ and K in the `pseudoXOF` calls, which the ruleset costs as ideal replacements by calls and bytes. Counted that way, with the input absorbed once and output in blocks of b bytes, key generation, encapsulation and decapsulation take 322 → 325, 401 → 404 to 406 and 407 → 410 to 412 SM3-compression equivalents at Lore-512 and b = 64, which with the same arithmetic model is +0.1% to +0.6% of each operation; at Lore-384 at most +0.8%; at b = 136, +0.2% or less. The SM3 backend's impact is therefore below the SHAKE backend's, which the Impact line gives. Alternative, not costed: in the helper as built, a 64-byte seed takes two SM3 compressions per 32-byte output block where a 32-byte seed takes one, and the matrix expansion recomputes its stream at every squeeze; counted as built, Lore-512 grows by +1.9% to +2.6%, +2.9% to +4.3% and +2.7% to +4.2%. If H(pk) and H(c) are also widened, the SM3 backend gives +1.9% to +16.3%, +3.0% to +26.6% and +2.7% to +24.7% at Lore-512 with the XOF idealized, and up to +12.1% as built; like the SHAKE alternative, that straddles 10%. Not checked: a measured profile, and the AVX and NEON trees. The model counts only the mod-t products as arithmetic. It leaves out the NTT part, the BCH codec and packing, and also the byte-wise absorb and squeeze code of the hash.
