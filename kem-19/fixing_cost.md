## Attacks considered

- kem-19-2: Lore-384 and -512 emit 256-bit keys from 256-bit keygen seeds (Design, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **kem-19-2.** Root cause: `LORE_SYMBYTES` is 32 at every level (`params.h:18`), so the key-generation seed, H(m), K̄, the coins r, z and K are all 256 bits, and §3.5 (physical p. 22) says that the 256-bit SHAKE and SM3 functions are temporary references at 384 and 512 bits, to be replaced. The plaintext is not the limit. m has κ = λ = 384 and 512 bits (Table 2, physical p. 29; `kem.c:163–164`) and the submitted BCH codes carry it, so no parameter, ciphertext field or failure probability changes. The costed repair gives the seed, seedsk, H(m), K̄, r, z and K λ bits, and moves the calls that take or produce them (Hgen, the secret sampler's XOF, H(m), G and KDF) to a hash of λ-bit strength. The specification names none, so the hash is costed by its dimensions as a Keccak sponge of capacity 2λ: rate 104 bytes at Lore-384 and 72 bytes at Lore-512, against 136 for the submitted SHAKE256 calls. expandA, H(pk) and H(c) take public inputs and stay as submitted, which is the cheapest form of the repair. Scope: Sub-algorithm, specification and code: the hash and XOF for secret values are replaced. Impact on sizes: none. The public key holds only the public 32-byte seedA and the ciphertext holds no hash value. The built KEM secret key grows by z, 16 and 32 bytes (under 1%). Impact on speed, SHAKE backend: Keccak permutations per key generation, encapsulation and decapsulation go from 129, 167 and 170 to 143, 193 and 197 at Lore-512, and from 90, 116 and 118 to 93, 121 and 123 at Lore-384. In the model below hashing is the smaller part of each operation, so the estimate is +2% to +5%, +2% to +7% and +2% to +6% at Lore-512, and under 3% at Lore-384. The lower end weighs a Keccak word operation like one 16-bit multiply or add of the mod-t product; the upper end weighs it four times as much, a judgment that allows for a compiler vectorising the multiplication loops. Encapsulation at Lore-512 reaches 10% at a weight of 10. Alternatives: if H(pk) and H(c) also move to the wider hash, the same model gives +4% to +11%, +6% to +17% and +5% to +15% at Lore-512, which straddles 10%. If the public matrix expansion moves as well, it gives +19% to +52%, +17% to +48% and +15% to +42%. How many of the public-input calls a revised design moves is what would tip the answer. SM3 backend: its XOF is the contest's `pseudoXOF` (`sm3.c:9–15`), which the ruleset costs as an ideal replacement by calls and bytes; the calls are the same in number, so the impact is none. Not counted there: in the helper as built, the 64-byte seeds of Lore-512 take two SM3 compressions per 32-byte block of sampler output where the 32-byte seeds take one. Not checked: a measured profile, and the AVX and NEON trees. The model counts only the mod-t products as arithmetic. It leaves out the NTT part, the BCH codec and packing, and also the byte-wise absorb and squeeze code of the hash.
