## Remediation

- **sign-28-1.** The root cause is that Table 5.3 takes τ·log2(N) + wgrind = λ + 2 while `chall3` has λ bits. Both implementations give the two missing bits to Δ and check wgrind − 2 zero bits, so the realized bound is 2^(λ−2) hash calls. The costed repair is the first proposal of the original post: lengthen `chall3` by ceil(log2 d) = 2 bits, so that Algorithms 2 and 3 check all wgrind zero bits and still pass τ·log2(N) bits to VOLE.DecodeAllChall. The post's second proposal, two more grinding bits, costs the same grinding. Its third, lowering the target by two bits, leaves the 256- and 512-bit sets under their levels and is not a repair. The team's 2026-10-09 response lowers Table 5.3 to the enforced values and argues from the bit-operation cost of one hash; it leaves the report's 2^(λ−2) count in place, so it is not the costed repair. That revision would change the specification only, with no impact. Scope: Function, specification and code: the length of `chall3` and its split in Algorithms 2 and 3 (physical pp. 16–17), and in the code the Δ length and the zero-bit count. The signature encoding changes: the `chall3` field grows by one byte. Impact on sizes: signature +1 byte in every set, which is +0.02% at 160s (count). Impact on signing: the code skipped specified grinding, so the corrected code does more. Expected `Hash3` calls rise from 2^(wgrind−2)/p to 2^wgrind/p, with p the opening probability of Table 5.5: 2,071 to 8,285 at 160s and 4,354 to 17,415 at 512s. Each call hashes the whole 213- or 653-byte input again. Set against the other hash and PRG work of one signature, the added calls are +9% to +20% at 512s and +5% to +25% at 160s (estimate), and under 1% at 256s and the three fast sets. Each range covers four cost models: one XOF byte costed as 1, 2 or 3 PRG bytes, and one SM3 compression call costed as one PRG block call. The middle models give +13% and +15% at 512s, and the answer follows them. Field arithmetic and data movement are not counted and lower every figure: if they are about a third or more of signing at 512s, the estimate falls under 10%. That share was not counted. The stated timings suggest it is large in the reference build and small in the AVX2 build, which makes the same pseudoXOF calls, so the case is borderline for the reference build and clearer for the AVX2 build. The answer would also change with an XOF that absorbs the fixed input prefix once per signature; the contest's one-shot pseudoXOF does not offer that and the submitted loop does not do it. Verification makes one `Hash3` call either way, and key generation does not grind. Giving the two added bits to Δ instead, as two trees of 2^12 leaves with the submitted grinding, adds 2/τ of the leaves: 14.3% at 160s and 4.3% at 512s, which is about +12% and +4% in the same four models. The submitted tree code takes its depths from the Δ length, but the specification defines its algorithms for one N and this is not a recorded proposal, so it is not the costed repair. It would cost less than the grinding at 512s. At 160s it is also above 10% in these models, so the answer does not depend on the choice. Its effect on the opening probability of Table 5.5 was not computed.
