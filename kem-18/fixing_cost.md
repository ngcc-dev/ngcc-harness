## Attacks considered

- kem-18-1: Partial rejection mask leaks the candidate shared secret (Implementation, Confirmed)
- kem-18-3: Public consistency equations recover Loong128, Loong256 and Loong384 shared secrets (Design, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **kem-18-1.** Expand the comparison result to `0x00` or `0xff` before the select, as the report states. The code negates an arbitrary nonzero byte (`KEM_Loong.c:302`). Algorithm 24 replaces the whole key on mismatch, so the specification is sound. Scope: Single line, code, in the twelve `KEM_Loong.c` files. Impact: none.
- **kem-18-3.** The report records no proposed fix. The attack cancels the block-negacyclic term of `U′` and is left with k2·N·(N−1) public equations in the k2·N short coefficients of `r2`: 48, 96 and 160 unknowns. The repair costed keeps the algorithms and changes the parameter tables: raise k2 until that instance reaches the level under both attacks that the specification counts. The specification already sets k2 by the cost of eliminating the unstructured components, but it counts brute force only; the repair counts lattice reduction there. The specification sets each level by the cheaper of a primal and a dual attack (§5.3.1–5.3.3 and Table 4, physical pp. 28–29), and its dual attack ends in recovering the secret (physical p. 28). The dual-hybrid estimate therefore applies to the same instance and the same goal, recovering `r2` and with it the shared secret. With the specification's own primal condition applied to the equations, the smallest k2 is 43 at Loong128 (submitted 4), 59 at Loong256 (6) and 72 at Loong384 (8). The dual-hybrid estimate is below the level at the last two, 2^246.0 and 2^364.1, and first reaches it at k2 = 62 (2^259.9) and k2 = 76 (2^386.6). At Loong128 it gives 2^133.9 at k2 = 43, so the primal bound decides there. Lowering k1 so that (k1 + k2)·N keeps its submitted value gives (k1, k2) = (9, 43), (8, 62) and (4, 76). The specification's failure formula depends only on that dimension, η, q and d_v (physical pp. 19–20), so the failure rate stays at its submitted value: the formula gives 2^-142.5, 2^-161.6 and 2^-190.3, against 2^-141, 2^-161 and 2^-190 in Table 2. Keeping k1 instead raises the formula to 2^-78.0, 2^-85.9 and 2^-98.6, outside the specification's own bound, so that variant would also need a new η or new compression values; it is not costed (its public key alone is +477%, +552% and +580%). In these three sets N is already the smallest value with N² ≥ λ, so it cannot be lowered to shrink the k2·N² term. Scope: Parameter set, specification and code (Table 2, the security analysis, and `K1`, `K2` and the length macros in `params.h`). Impact: public key +437%, +517% and +551% and ciphertext +426%, +477% and +532% at Loong128, Loong256 and Loong384. The public matrix expanded in every operation grows 8.0, 11.5 and 15.2 times and the multiplications of one encryption 7.9, 11.3 and 15.0 times; the three operations have nearly equal stated timings, so each grows by about that factor. This is a lower bound. The models ignore the quotient projection that the Loong384 attack uses and the correlation between equations, both of which help the attacker. The attack was not rerun at the new parameters, and the specification's Table 4 analysis of the whole instance was not redone; its dimension (k1 + k2)·N is unchanged. The quotient model of kem-18-2 (High, Lead, not costed) gives no finite cost at (9, 43) and (8, 72), where the ring rows supply 9 and 8 polynomial samples for 52 and 80 polynomial unknowns. At (4, 76) it has 4 samples for the same 80 unknowns; the estimator uses at most the samples it is given, so the cost cannot fall, but it was not rerun. The unstructured block becomes most of the dimension, which moves the candidate towards an unstructured LWE scheme; the specification's own comparison lists FrodoKEM-640 at 9,616 and 9,720 bytes. A change to the construction that stops the cancellation is not proposed anywhere and is not costed. Loong512 is not in the finding and is not costed.
- **kem-18-4.** Compute the rejection key over the received ciphertext, `H(salt‖c)`, as Algorithm 24 line 4 specifies and the report states. The code re-encrypts into the hashed buffer (`:289`) and hashes `salt‖c′` (`:295`). Scope: Function (`kem_dec`), code. Impact: none. The code already hashes `SEED_BYTES + CIPHERTEXT_BYTES` bytes once per call; the repair changes which ciphertext is in that buffer. The re-encryption then needs its own buffer, 1,512 to 10,848 bytes of stack.
