No: one global Reed–Solomon code, the team's repair, is encoded in its revised code by a transform over cosets of an order-51 subgroup that takes 1.97, 1.49 and 1.11 times the field multiplications but 1.32, 1.00 and 0.74 times the table lookups of the submitted 32-chunk encoding at Sigurd-128, -256 and -512, and the team's revised timings put reference signing at +1.4% at Sigurd-128 and lower elsewhere; the nonzero-ζ rule adds no work.

Impact: signing +1.4% at Sigurd-128 (judgment)

## Attacks considered

- sign-24-1: Chunked Reed–Solomon encoding reveals the signing witness (Implementation, Confirmed)
- sign-24-2: Zero 16-bit challenge exposes the signing witness (Implementation, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **sign-24-1.** The root cause is that `RS_encode` splits the padded vector into 32 chunks and encodes each as its own short code, so the random tail does not hide the early chunks. The specification commits to one codeword of the whole padded vector (Algorithm 4.1, physical p. 19). The costed repair is the team's: one global code of dimension N′ and length N in every parameter set, with the evaluation domain stated. The signature format does not change, because N, Q_VC and the Merkle layout stay. The revised code (read 2026-11-24 at commit `302eeeb`, encoder unchanged since the first commit of 2026-09-23) evaluates the one polynomial on the first ⌈N/51⌉ cosets of the order-51 subgroup of GF(2^16)*: per lane and coset it folds the N′ coefficients into 51 values, multiplies by a chirp and applies a 51-point transform made of 17 three-point and 3 direct 17-point DFTs (`rsencode.c`, `rs_eval_codeword`). It works on the coefficients directly, so no basis conversion is needed. Scope: Sub-algorithm, specification and code. The encoder in `rsencode.c` is replaced in the reference and optimized trees, `RS_encode` loses its chunking, and the specification fixes the domain L, which it leaves abstract. Impact, per call (count): 427,180, 2,697,776 and 27,696,800 multiplications, 1.97, 1.49 and 1.11 times the submitted call, and about as many additions. Its products cost two table lookups (`gf_mul_log`) against three in the submitted `gf_mul`, so the lookups are 1.32, 1.00 and 0.74 times the submitted ones. Tables add 33,341 two-byte entries, and their setup is 33,001 products once per process, not per call. Signing makes two calls and the reference verifier one. The rise in signing is the share of the encoding in signing times that ratio. That share was not counted: apart from the encoding, signing at Sigurd-128 performs about 468,720 products in `Initial_Computation_Prover_Raw` and about 78,400 in `Commitment_Evals_Raw`, besides the Merkle hashing and the sampling. So the encoding is at most about 44% of signing's products, and signing rises by between 0% and +43% under a count of products, or between 0% and +14% under a count of lookups. The range straddles 10%. The team's revised specification in the same repository (dated 2026-09-21, Table 3, reference build) gives signing 16.41, 136.18 and 1149.03 Mcyc and verification 7.00, 46.63 and 348.35 Mcyc, which is +1.4%, −3.2% and −10.0% and +0.3%, −10.6% and −24.4% against the submitted Table 3 (as reported, not evaluated). The header takes +1.4% at Sigurd-128 as the central estimate, by judgment. It would tip to Yes if the timed build was not the global-code encoder, or if the encoding took more than about a third of the lookups of signing at Sigurd-128. Its Table 4 gives the optimized verifier +10.6% and +12.4% at -128 and -256; that verifier evaluates only the opened positions, and the optimized build is context, not the baseline. Alternatives: a radix-2 additive transform of length 2^11, 2^13 and 2^15 would need 0.52, 0.47 and 0.32 of the submitted multiplications, but its additions (1.04, 0.94 and 0.63 of the call for the butterflies alone), the conversion from the coefficient basis of Algorithm 4.1 and its tables were not bounded, so it is not used for the answer. Keeping the Horner loop on the global code is also a Sub-algorithm change of the encoder and the domain. One call then costs 6,146,560 multiplications at 128 (×28.4), 54,246,528 at 256 (×30.0) and 785,981,952 at 512 (×31.5) (count), and the answer would be Yes on that path. Not checked: the revised specification in the repository does not state the domain, although the team's post says the revised specification does; nothing was built, run or timed.
- **sign-24-2.** The root cause is that ζ is one 16-bit word that may be zero, which removes the mask ζ·F from the response G = E + ζ·F. The costed repair is the team's proposal: take the first nonzero word of the `polyres` stream and state that rule in the specification. Scope: Single line, specification and code (`hash_to_rand_PolyRes`; Algorithm 6.2, physical p. 34, gives neither the width of ζ nor a nonzero rule). Impact: none (count). A word is zero with probability 2^-16, so signing and verification read 2^-16 extra words on average. The team reports unchanged serialized formats and unchanged test vectors (as reported, not evaluated). The repair keeps ζ at 16 bits; the report records no attack on a nonzero ζ, and none was looked for here.
