Yes: the only proposed repair of the round-key cancellation, a relocated key-expansion S-box, is recorded as still giving collisions in all six instances, so the key expansion has to be redesigned and the cost per block cannot be bounded.

Impact: not bounded
Scope: Construction (large), specification and code
Assessed: 2026-11-23

## Attacks considered

- hash-19-1: Round-key cancellation gives deterministic full-round collisions (Design, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **hash-19-1.** The root cause is in the key expansion (Section 2.4.4, Algorithms 3 and 4). Each 576-bit sub-block of the message is expanded on its own by one S-box row, a word transposition and rotations, and the round key is the XOR of the sub-blocks. Words 5 and 7 are moved identically in the sub-blocks, never enter the S-box and are rotated by odd amounts, so a two-periodic difference placed in both cancels from every round key. The first candidate is the submitters' revision: the S-box acts on the first row of the first block and on the second row of the second block. It adds no word operation, because the number of S-box calls per round is unchanged, and the submitters call the overhead negligible. It would be a Function change, specification and code, with impact none. It is not relied on, because the report records an attack against it: ePrint 2026/2886 reports that the revised reference code keeps the per-round cancellation and gives explicit collisions for all six revised variants. The report records that claim without evaluating it, and notes that it concerns the revised code and not the revised specification. No other fix is recorded. A repair then needs a new key expansion in which no message difference cancels from every round key, and a differential analysis that covers it: Section 6.2.1 ties key collisions to related-key trails, and Section 6.2.3 analyzes the cipher state only. That changes the compression function. Scope: Construction, specification and code. Impact: not bounded, because no such key expansion is proposed or costed. For context, the key expansion is 31 of the 95 word operations per round in the DM instances and 31 of 104 in the MDPH instances, and the specification kept it light for speed. A heavier expansion would add work in proportion to that share, and a change of the transposition or rotation tables alone would add none. Neither is proposed, and an assessor's choice between them is not a basis for an answer. If the submitters' revision, or another change of that size, were shown to remove the cancellation, the answer would be `No` with scope Function.

## Evidence

- Baseline: all six instances use the 576-bit cipher with 16 rounds (Table 1, physical p. 5) and 1152-bit message blocks (Algorithm 5 line 8, physical p. 17; Algorithm 6 line 12, physical p. 18). The DM instances (MoFang-256, -512 and -256-XOF) make one cipher call per block. The MDPH instances (MoFang-768, -1024 and -768-XOF) make two (Algorithm 6 lines 3 and 4), and the reference code runs the key expansion in each (`MoFang/Implementations/Reference_Implementation/MoFang-1024/CryptHash_AlgorithmInstance.c:193-228`). The digest lengths are fixed and a hash has no bandwidth. Tables 9 and 10 (physical p. 30) give throughput; they are context and were not used.
- Key expansion as specified: text on physical p. 12-13, Tables 5 and 6 (physical p. 13), Algorithm 3 (physical p. 14) and Algorithm 4 (physical p. 15). In Table 5 the entries for words (1,2) and (2,1) are (2,1) and (1,2) for every block, so each block swaps these two words. The design rationale keeps the expansion light: "we discard the computationally heavy MDS matrix" (physical p. 12) and "we apply S-box operation for the first row only in three" to maintain performance (Section 5.2.2, physical p. 23).
- Count, reference MoFang-512 (`MoFang/Implementations/Reference_Implementation/MoFang-512/CryptHash_AlgorithmInstance.c`), 64-bit word operations per round with word moves not counted. State path: three S-box calls of 6 operations = 18 (`:59-80`), `MixColumns` 19 XOR (`:106-116`), 9 rotations (`:118-130`), `AddM_Key1` 18 XOR (`:83-89`); 64 in all. Key expansion (`:133-162`): two S-box calls = 12, 18 rotations, 1 constant XOR; 31 in all. Total 95, and 31/95 = 33%.
- Count, reference MoFang-1024 (same tree, `MoFang-1024/CryptHash_AlgorithmInstance.c`): `AddM_Key2` has 27 XOR (`:124-130`), so the state path is 73. The first sub-block is only transposed and the other two take one S-box call and 9 rotations each (`:133-175`), so the key expansion is again 31. Total 104, and 31/104 = 30%.
- Proposed fix: the [revision notice](https://list.niccs.org.cn/archives/list/crypthashforum@list.niccs.org.cn/message/MKUGDOEMKQFY4M7UWJT5EF5AMOCUKITQ/) moves the S-box of the second block to the second row and states "The performance overhead is negligible", as reported by the submitters and not evaluated. By count it leaves the 12 S-box operations of the key expansion per round unchanged.
- Analysis in the specification: Section 6.2.1 (physical p. 24) says that for a key collision "high-probability related-key differential trails are required". Section 6.2.3 (physical p. 25-26) counts active S-boxes in trails of the 576-bit cipher state and gives no trail through the key expansion.
- Recorded attack on the proposal: the abstract of [ePrint 2026/2886](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2886) says that moving the S-box of the second message half to row 1 "does not eliminate the original per-round key cancellation", as recorded in the report's `Proposed fixes` subsection. In the paper, Theorem 2.1 gives 16 inputs with equal round keys whenever six words of the second half are zero, Tables 3 and 4 give a 144-byte pair with a common digest in each of the six revised reference variants, and Section 6.2 says the results follow the revised code and not the letter of the revised specification. Section 7 says that "merely moving the S-box does not eliminate the per-round key cancellation" and that message expansions should avoid symmetries such as the period-two masks under odd rotations; it names no replacement key expansion. These are the paper's claims and were not reproduced.
- Not checked: the erratum and updated specification attached to the notice, the submitters' repository, and the appendices of ePrint 2026/2886 were not read. The optimized and vector code was not counted. No candidate key expansion was analyzed.
