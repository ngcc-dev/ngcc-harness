## Attacks considered

- sign-32-1: Every signature is accepted (Implementation, Confirmed)
- sign-32-3: Pair-correlated signatures recover an equivalent UVW-128 or UVW-256 signing key (Design, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **sign-32-1.** The API wrapper stores the verdict of `uvw_verify` and then returns 0 (`Reference_Implementation/UVW-128/SIG_AlgorithmInstance.c:619–623`; the other five wrappers do the same). The costed repair returns the stored result, as the report states. Scope: Single line, code. Algorithm 8 (physical p. 14) is sound and stays. Impact: none. `uvw_verify` already computes e·R^T and both weights (`uvw.c:507–533`), so the code skipped no specified work.
- **sign-32-3.** The root cause is the sampler. Algorithm 5 step 3 (p. 10) makes both members of every hidden pair nonzero on the information set, and the only test is on the total weight, so the distribution of a signature depends on the secret pairing. A key-use limit is not a repair, because the recovery used about 300 signatures. The report records no proposed fix. The costed repair is the rejection sampling of Wave, reference [5] of the specification, whose aim is an output that is uniform over the words of weight w and so does not depend on the key. UVW signs with the same two decoders, e2 first and then e1 with e = (e1 ‖ e1 + e2) up to the monomial (p. 17). It needs three changes. (1) D2, which Algorithm 4 leaves open, is fixed so that |e2| follows Proposition 3 (p. 23), with a rejection test on |e2|. (2) DecXY draws the number of information-set positions inside the support of e2 and adds a rejection test on the number of all-zero pairs. (3) A gap g separates k1 from the number of forced positions, as in Wave; the repository's decoding note describes the submitted parameters as the zero-gap setting (`sign-32/decoding_analysis.md` §2.1). We cost g as the smallest value with 3^-g ≤ 2^-λ (81, 162, 324), which is our assumption, moved from k2 to k1 with n, k and w unchanged. Scope: Parameter set, specification and code. Algorithms 4, 5 and 7, Theorem 2 and the k1 and k2 columns of Table 1 change, with `dec_z` and `dec_xy`. No encoding changes. Impact on sizes: none, because the signature and the public key depend only on n and k (p. 11). Impact on signing, counted on the matrix inversions that dominate it: the gap alone gives +5.3% to +5.4%, because DecZ inverts a matrix of side n/2 − k2 + g; with both rejection tests at the acceptance rates found by the script (at least 0.985 and 0.96) it is +7.0% to +7.2% (estimate), provided the weight loop of DecXY keeps one information set, as Algorithm 5 and the submitted `dec_xy` do. Whether a Wave-style sampler may do so is not settled here. The text of p. 10 describes the same loop as "retrying by selecting new information sets", and the proof of Theorem 2 (pp. 19–20) treats its trials as independent. Keeping one set makes the accepted output the conditional distribution of one trial at that set, while new sets weight each set by its acceptance probability; the two agree only under a heuristic that was not checked, and the uniformity argument of Wave was not redone for either. With a new information set, hence a new inversion, for every weight trial, signing becomes 6.15, 8.26 and 11.20 times the submitted inversion work, +515%, +726% and +1,020% (estimate). The impact therefore ranges from +7% to +1,020%, which straddles 10%. Neither reading is weighed above the other, and every central estimate of that range, such as the geometric mean of the two ratios (2.6 to 3.5 times), is above 10%, so the answer is Yes. A second-stage rejection that also repeated DecZ would give +9% to +12% at the low end. Verification: none. Key generation: under 1% (judgment), because the largest elimination has n − k rows and does not change. A smaller gap would lower the low end: the Wave I set of the Wave NIST submission (https://wave-sign.org/), as listed in `sign-32/decoding/keyatk.py` and `cost.py`, has (n, w, kU) = (8,576, 7,668, 2,966), a gap of 40 under the same relation, and g = 40 gives +2.6% at UVW128 for the gap alone. Not checked: no sampler was written or run; the uniformity argument was not redone for UVW; whether the weight loop may reuse its information set, and whether a cheaper re-randomisation of the information set than a full inversion would keep the output uniform; the text of Wave's own decoder, which is not in the repository; whether a gap is needed at all; the Wave I values against the Wave specification; quantum costs.

## Evidence

- Baseline sizes: Table 1 (physical p. 15) states signatures of 977, 1,934 and 3,928 bytes; |σ| = λ + k·log2 3 bits (p. 11) gives 7,815.1, 15,471.6 and 31,418.8 bits, which is the same. Table 3 (p. 35) gives the stored sizes: signatures of 1,244, 2,444 and 4,956 bytes and public keys of 5,897,612, 23,040,012 and 95,160,012 bytes. Both tables depend on n and k only.
- Baseline work: signing inverts one square matrix of side n/2 − k2 in `dec_z` (`uvw.c:149–242`) and one of side n/2 − k1 in `dec_xy`, then repeats two vector-matrix products until the weight is w (`uvw.c:244–355`). Each inversion is retried on new columns until the matrix is invertible (`uvw.c:188–193,251–289`), which scales the submitted and the repaired work alike. Sides: 3,250 and 1,600; 6,433 and 3,167; 13,066 and 6,434. Inversion work is taken as the cube of the side, so DecZ is 89.3% of it. Theorem 2 (p. 19) gives 66.9, 94.1 and 134.1 loop iterations; their products are 0.5% or less of the inversion count. The set-up that rebuilds the key from its seed is not repeated on a rejection and is not counted. Nothing was measured.
- `python3 -B sign-32/fixing_cost_sampler.py` (about one minute) prints the figures below. It implements no sampler.
- Information sets: Algorithm 5 (physical p. 10) draws I once before the repeat loop, and the submitted `dec_xy` keeps one set while it redraws x (`uvw.c:244–355`); the paragraph above Algorithm 4 on the same page says the loop retries "by selecting new information sets until the total weight matches perfectly", and the proof of Theorem 2 (pp. 19–20) takes the candidates as statistically independent.
- Gap: (k1, k2) becomes (3,331, 1,519), (6,595, 3,005) and (13,390, 6,110). The forced positions and the expected weight (2/3)(n + k1 − g) are unchanged. Inverted sides become 3,331 and 1,519; 6,595 and 3,005; 13,390 and 6,110. (3,331^3 + 1,519^3)/(3,250^3 + 1,600^3) = 1.053.

Both decoders are narrower than their targets, so a mixing distribution can match mean and variance.

A stage-1 rejection is taken to repeat DecZ and a stage-2 rejection to repeat DecXY, each with a new information set.
