## Attacks considered

- sign-32-1: Every signature is accepted (Implementation, Confirmed)
- sign-32-3: Pair-correlated signatures recover an equivalent UVW-128 or UVW-256 signing key (Design, Confirmed)

## Remediation

- **sign-32-1.** The API wrapper stores the verdict of `uvw_verify` and then returns 0 (`Reference_Implementation/UVW-128/SIG_AlgorithmInstance.c:619–623`; the other five wrappers do the same). The costed repair returns the stored result, as the report states. Scope: Single line, code. Algorithm 8 (physical p. 14) is sound and stays. Impact: none. `uvw_verify` already computes e·R^T and both weights (`uvw.c:507–533`), so the code skipped no specified work.
- **sign-32-3.** The root cause is the sampler. Algorithm 5 step 3 (p. 10) makes both members of every hidden pair nonzero on the information set, and the only test is on the total weight, so the distribution of a signature depends on the secret pairing. A key-use limit is not a repair, because the recovery used about 300 signatures. The report records no proposed fix. The costed repair is the rejection sampling of Wave, reference [5] of the specification, whose aim is an output that is uniform over the words of weight w and so does not depend on the key. UVW signs with the same two decoders, e2 first and then e1 with e = (e1 ‖ e1 + e2) up to the monomial (p. 17). It needs three changes. (1) D2, which Algorithm 4 leaves open, is fixed so that |e2| follows Proposition 3 (p. 23), with a rejection test on |e2|. (2) DecXY draws the number of information-set positions inside the support of e2 and adds a rejection test on the number of all-zero pairs. (3) A gap g separates k1 from the number of forced positions, as in Wave; the repository's decoding note describes the submitted parameters as the zero-gap setting (`sign-32/decoding_analysis.md` §2.1). We cost g as the smallest value with 3^-g ≤ 2^-λ (81, 162, 324), which is our assumption, moved from k2 to k1 with n, k and w unchanged. Scope: Parameter set, specification and code. Algorithms 4, 5 and 7, Theorem 2 and the k1 and k2 columns of Table 1 change, with `dec_z` and `dec_xy`. No encoding changes. Impact on sizes: none, because the signature and the public key depend only on n and k (p. 11). Impact on signing, counted on the matrix inversions that dominate it: the gap alone gives +5.3% to +5.4%, because DecZ inverts a matrix of side n/2 − k2 + g; with both rejection tests at the acceptance rates found by the script (at least 0.985 and 0.96) it is +7.0% to +7.2% (estimate). Without a gap it would be +1.6% to +1.8%. Verification: none. Key generation: under 1% (judgment), because the largest elimination has n − k rows and does not change. An estimate between 5% and 10% is borderline. We answer No because the percentages count only the inversions, which are less than the whole operation, the acceptance rates are lower bounds from a simple search, and no size moves. It would tip to Yes if a second-stage rejection had to repeat DecZ as well (+9% to +12%), if the first-stage acceptance fell below about 0.96, if the designers chose a larger gap, or if the weight loop could not keep one information set as the submitted `dec_xy` does: a new inversion for every weight trial would make the inversion work about 6, 8 and 11 times the submitted work. A smaller gap would lower the figure: the Wave I set of the Wave NIST submission (https://wave-sign.org/), as listed in `sign-32/decoding/keyatk.py` and `cost.py`, has (n, w, kU) = (8,576, 7,668, 2,966), a gap of 40 under the same relation, and g = 40 gives +2.6% at UVW128 for the gap alone. Not checked: no sampler was written or run; the uniformity argument was not redone for UVW; whether the weight loop may reuse its information set; whether a gap is needed at all; the Wave I values against the Wave specification; quantum costs.

## Evidence

Each inversion is retried on new columns until the matrix is invertible (`uvw.c:188–193,251–289`), which scales the submitted and the repaired work alike. Sides: 3,250 and 1,600; 6,433 and 3,167; 13,066 and 6,434. Inversion work is taken as the cube of the side, so DecZ is 89.3% of it. Theorem 2 (p. 19) gives 66.9, 94.1 and 134.1 loop iterations; their products are 0.5% or less of the inversion count. The set-up that rebuilds the key from its seed is not repeated on a rejection and is not counted. Nothing was measured.
- `python3 -B sign-32/fixing_cost_sampler.py` (about one minute) prints the figures below. It implements no sampler.
- Gap: (k1, k2) becomes (3,331, 1,519), (6,595, 3,005) and (13,390, 6,110). The forced positions and the expected weight (2/3)(n + k1 − g) are unchanged. Inverted sides become 3,331 and 1,519; 6,595 and 3,005; 13,390 and 6,110. (3,331^3 + 1,519^3)/(3,250^3 + 1,600^3) = 1.053.
